## 1. Executive summary

Opralvin One, as a recommendation and not a locked decision, is for an Indian firm inside the owner's ₹2 Cr to ₹200 Cr band that has outgrown a desktop accounts package plus spreadsheets and does not want a long enterprise project.

The recommended first call is a trading firm with more than one godown, still run by the owner and one accountant. That is a calling order, not proof of demand (L46). The score is 15, the highest in `docs/strategy/icp-scoring.csv`. The wide band stays the owner's brief. The tighter slice is qualitative. It is not a new rupee range.

The offer ladder is four scoped offers plus the CA referral: one free Process Audit, three paid offers (Starter Onboarding, Growth Implementation, Managed Opralvin One), and the CA partner referral. The audit is the primary call to action.

The owner still has to pick: this positioning, the prices behind `{{STARTER_PRICE}}`, `{{GROWTH_PRICE}}`, `{{MANAGED_PRICE}}`, `{{DAY_RATE}}`, and `{{CA_FEE}}`, the India Compliance revision, and the flagship modules. Phase 2 does not start until those conversations and that pick.

## 3. Competitive frame

Prices below are copied from `docs/strategy/competitors.csv`. Each figure has a URL and the retrieval date 2026-10-25 in section 12. "When we are the wrong choice" is the last column of that CSV and is repeated here.

TallyPrime's strength is that the accountant often already knows it. As listed on 2026-10-25, excluding GST, Silver lifetime is ₹22500 and Gold lifetime is ₹67500, each plus 18% GST on the buy page. We are the wrong choice when the firm wants that company file read in automatically. The cost of having no Tally company reader is a manual move: spreadsheet import, opening invoices, opening stock, and a chart of accounts (L16, L26, L28, L29). Firms that will not do that move should stay on TallyPrime.

Zoho Books' strength is a packaged cloud subscription with a free plan while financial-year revenue does not exceed 25 lakhs, and paid plans at ₹749, ₹1499, and ₹2999 per organisation per month billed annually, plus Elite at ₹4999 per organisation per month. GST treatment is not stated on the page. We are the wrong choice when that subscription covers the job and the firm does not want an implementation.

Zoho Inventory's India pricing URL did not state an India rupee price on 2026-10-25. The response was in dollars and marked for a US-region offer, so no dollar figure is used as an India price. The strength we can state is packaged inventory. We are the wrong choice when that package is enough.

Busy's strength is a known Indian package with GST features on the paid plans. As listed on 2026-10-25, excluding GST: Basic ₹11000, Standard ₹16500, Enterprise ₹22000, and Magic plans at ₹5000, ₹8000, ₹12000, and ₹50000 per 360 days. We are the wrong choice when that package already does the job.

Marg ERP publishes Nano ₹5550, Basic ₹10300, Silver ₹13900, and Gold ₹26000, with 18% GST extra. We are the wrong choice when that licence is enough.

Vyapar shows ₹242 per month. GST treatment is not stated. A struck ₹350 is on the page and the asterisk is not explained, so it is not treated as a second price. We are the wrong choice for a smaller counter that wants that billing app.

SAP Business One's page describes one system for accounting, purchasing, inventory, sales, and reporting. Price is not stated. We are the wrong choice when the firm wants that vendor and a partner-led project.

Odoo's page lists One App Free, Standard at US$31.10 per user per month, and Custom at US$61.00 per user per month, and it says implementation is not included. GST treatment is not stated. We are the wrong choice when the firm has chosen that subscription and another implementer.

Other implementation shops are a category with no firm names. Their strength is custom work in the same city. Their price is not stated, because naming a shop or inventing a quote would be a fake competitor. We are the wrong choice when the firm wants bespoke software rather than a scoped setup.

The cost of no Tally reader is not waved away. It is a reason some firms should not buy.

## 4. Positioning statement

Recommendation, not a lock: Opralvin One is for an Indian firm, roughly ₹2 Cr to ₹200 Cr, that has outgrown a desktop accounts package plus spreadsheets and does not want a long enterprise project. The first work is the books, the stock, and the GST paperwork they already have to file. The data move is spreadsheet import, opening invoices, opening stock, and a chart of accounts. There is no Tally company reader.

Differentiation, also a recommendation: what this firm does, and a packaged product does not, is write the scope and the refusals before the build, then do the books, stock, and GST paperwork on the system the firm can export (L27). What a generic implementation shop often does not do is put those refusals in the offer before the days start. Whether that reason scales past two people is untested. The test is in section 11.

## 7. Offer architecture

Four scoped offers plus the CA referral. Not five software editions.

Shared product ban list, written in scope-out for Starter Onboarding, Growth Implementation, and Managed Opralvin One only: a Tally company reader, e-invoice or e-way from a sales order, GSTR-3B portal filing, an owner phone app for stock, sales, and receivables, a verified UPI flow, CRM lead fields for UTM, consent, score, or a calculator result, automatic backups, PF or ESI deducted on the slip, and a live GST portal demo until the owner names an India Compliance revision that boots.

### Process Audit

Scope in: read how the firm invoices, holds stock, and files GST, and write what is inside the ledger and what is not.
Scope out: no build, no data move, and no price.
Timeline: set when the audit is booked. No public day count.
Deliverables: a written note of the current path and of what we will not take on.
Client duties: one person who knows the books, plus sample invoices and a stock list they are willing to show.
Acceptance: the note is delivered to that person.
Exit and data ownership: the firm keeps its files. We do not keep a copy of their books after the note is delivered unless they ask in writing.
Price field: free, no charge. No price token.

### Starter Onboarding

Scope in: one company, the India chart of accounts, opening invoices, opening stock, users who can raise an invoice and see stock (L16, L22, L26, L28, L29, L04, L14).
Scope out: the shared ban list, plus no multi-godown rollout and no GST portal work.
Timeline: `{{DAY_RATE}}` times the days in the statement of work. The day count is agreed there. It is not published here.
Deliverables: a company that can raise an invoice and show stock, and an export of the masters (L27).
Client duties: the spreadsheet of customers, items, opening invoices, and opening stock, and a person who can sign off.
Acceptance: the named users raise one invoice and open the stock balance without us driving the screen.
Exit and data ownership: the firm keeps the company data and the export. We do not keep a copy unless they ask in writing.
Pricing logic: `{{STARTER_PRICE}}` equals `{{DAY_RATE}}` times the agreed days. No rupee amount.

### Growth Implementation

Scope in: Starter Onboarding, plus purchase orders, goods inward, more than one godown, and the GST paperwork in L34, L35, L36, and L37. Not a live demo (L45).
Scope out: the shared ban list.
Timeline: `{{DAY_RATE}}` times the days in the statement of work.
Deliverables: the starter company, plus purchase, godowns, and a written GST limit that says what is a file and what is not a portal demo.
Client duties: GST credentials if they want GSTR-1 filing tested, a person who files today, and the opening data.
Acceptance: the named users complete one purchase receipt, see stock in two godowns, and the GST limit note is signed. A live portal demo is not the acceptance test.
Exit and data ownership: same as Starter Onboarding.
Pricing logic: `{{GROWTH_PRICE}}` equals `{{DAY_RATE}}` times the agreed days. No rupee amount.

### Managed Opralvin One

Scope in: a named number of days each month after Growth Implementation, for questions, corrections, and the next list both sides sign.
Scope out: the shared ban list. Management is not a new product promise.
Timeline: month to month. The day count is in the statement of work.
Deliverables: the days, and a short note of what changed.
Client duties: one contact, and access that stays theirs.
Acceptance: the days were used on the signed list, or were released back if the firm did not book them.
Exit and data ownership: the firm keeps the data and the export. Stopping the month stops our access.
Pricing logic: `{{MANAGED_PRICE}}` equals `{{DAY_RATE}}` times the agreed days in the month. No rupee amount.

### CA partner referral

Who it is for: a firm whose chartered accountant already files, or who asks us for an introduction.
What we do: make the introduction and explain the offer limits.
What we never do: no software delivery, no invented fee, and no other firm's name. This field is the scope-out. There is no separate scope-out heading.
How a referral is handed over: the accountant and the firm both have the written offer. We do not start a build from the introduction alone.
Fee: `{{CA_FEE}}` until the owner sets one.

## 9. Funnel and KPIs

The free Process Audit stays the primary call to action.

Why it is free: the firm will not pay to discover a refusal. The audit is how we refuse a Tally reader, a GST demo, or a phone app before anyone is billed (L39, L40, L45).

What it qualifies: that the first work is books, stock, and GST paperwork inside the ledger, and that the firm will accept a spreadsheet move.

The two-person capacity ceiling: two people cannot run an unlimited number of audits. This choice is an assumption (L47). The test is in section 11.

Events, named so a later tracking plan can use them: `audit_page_view`, `audit_form_start`, `audit_form_submit`, `whatsapp_click`, `offer_starter_view`, `offer_growth_view`, `offer_managed_view`, `ca_referral_handover`.

Rates are assumptions. A booked-audit rate, a qualified rate, and a paid-offer rate will be counted from those events for 90 days and then revised. No target percentage is set, because a target percentage would be an invented metric.

## 10. 90-day go-to-market

No paid spend. No public send. No posts.

Days 1 to 30: the owner has the discovery conversations in the decision pack. The audit is offered only to people the owner already knows. The CA referral is mentioned in the same conversations and is not the thing we ask for first.

Days 31 to 60: compare the two paths. Count conversations that started from the free audit, and conversations that started from a CA introduction. The comparison is an assumption (L48). The test is in section 11. The audit stays the primary path even if the CA count is higher in this window. A switch would be a new owner decision, not a silent change.

Days 61 to 90: write what the conversations showed into the owner list. Do not start Phase 2 inside this window.

A two-person team can do the conversations, the notes, and the offer drafts. It cannot do ads, a city-page set, or a launch film.

## 11. Risks

The three weakest assumptions, and the test for each:

1. Trading and distribution is the right first call (L46). Test: the owner holds Phase 2 until a few discovery conversations confirm the pain. If they do not, the beachhead changes before any page is designed.
2. The free audit is the right primary call, and two people can carry it (L47). Test: count booked audits for 30 days and stop booking when both people are already in audits. If the calendar fills in the first week, the audit stops being offered as unbounded.
3. The CA referral will not replace the audit as the primary path (L48). Test: the 90-day count in section 10. The audit stays primary unless the owner changes it after seeing the count.

Other risks: a page that promises a GST demo, a rupee price typed in place of a token, or a client name from the bench. The checklist is the control. The version mismatch means GST rows stay Demo-able N (L45).
